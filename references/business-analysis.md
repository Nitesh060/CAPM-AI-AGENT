# Business Analysis & Value Delivery

## Overview
Business analysis focuses on identifying business needs, defining requirements, and ensuring projects deliver value aligned with organizational strategy.

## Business Analyst Role in Projects
The business analyst (or PM performing BA functions) bridges the gap between stakeholders' needs and the solution delivered.

**Key Responsibilities:**
- Elicit and document requirements
- Analyze business needs
- Validate solutions meet requirements
- Facilitate stakeholder communication
- Support change management

## Needs Assessment
The process of determining business needs and recommending viable solutions.

**Steps:**
1. Identify problem/opportunity
2. Assess current state
3. Determine future state (desired outcome)
4. Conduct gap analysis
5. Recommend solution approach

## Business Case
A documented justification for a proposed project, demonstrating value and feasibility.

**Typical Components:**
- Business need/problem statement
- Situation analysis
- Options and recommendations
- Cost-benefit analysis
- ROI/financial analysis

## Financial Analysis Concepts

### Return on Investment (ROI)
ROI = (Net Profit / Cost of Investment) × 100

### Net Present Value (NPV)
The value of future cash flows discounted to present value. NPV > 0 means project is financially viable.

### Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
The discount rate at which NPV = 0. Higher IRR = more attractive investment.

### Payback Period
Time required to recover the cost of investment. Shorter payback = lower risk.

### Benefit-Cost Ratio (BCR)
BCR = Present Value of Benefits / Present Value of Costs
BCR > 1 means benefits exceed costs.

### Opportunity Cost
The value of the best alternative foregone when choosing one option over another.

**Real-World Example:**
If a company can invest in Project A (NPV $50,000) or Project B (NPV $80,000) but can only choose one, the opportunity cost of choosing A is $80,000 (the forgone value of B).

## Requirements Management

### Requirements Types
- **Business Requirements**: High-level organizational needs
- **Stakeholder Requirements**: Needs of specific stakeholder groups
- **Solution Requirements**: Functional and non-functional details
  - **Functional**: What the system should do
  - **Non-functional**: Quality attributes (performance, security, usability)
- **Transition Requirements**: Temporary capabilities needed to move from current to future state

### Requirements Elicitation Techniques
- **Interviews**: One-on-one discussions with stakeholders
- **Focus Groups**: Facilitated discussion with pre-qualified stakeholders
- **Workshops**: Facilitated sessions to define requirements collaboratively
- **Observation (Job Shadowing)**: Watching users perform tasks
- **Surveys/Questionnaires**: Collecting data from large groups
- **Prototyping**: Creating early models for feedback
- **Document Analysis**: Reviewing existing documentation
- **Brainstorming**: Generating ideas in a group setting

### Requirements Traceability Matrix (RTM)
A document that links requirements throughout the project lifecycle — from origin to delivery — ensuring each requirement adds business value and is tested/validated.

## Product Roadmap
A high-level visual summary that maps out the vision and direction of a product offering over time.

## Product vs Project Life Cycle
- **Product Life Cycle**: Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline (ongoing, spans multiple projects)
- **Project Life Cycle**: Initiating → Planning → Executing → Closing (temporary, one iteration)

A single product may be supported by many projects throughout its life cycle (e.g., new feature releases, upgrades).

## Systems Thinking
Viewing the organization/project as an interconnected system where changes in one area affect others. Helps PMs understand ripple effects of decisions.

## Organizational Strategy Alignment
Projects should support organizational strategy through:
- **Strategic Plan**: Long-term organizational direction
- **Portfolio**: Collection of projects/programs aligned with strategic objectives
- **Program**: Group of related projects managed together for benefits not available from managing individually
- **Project**: Individual temporary endeavor supporting program/portfolio goals

**Hierarchy**: Strategy → Portfolio → Program → Project

## Change Management (Organizational)
The process of preparing, supporting, and helping individuals, teams, and organizations make organizational change.

**ADKAR Model** (common framework referenced):
- **A**wareness of the need for change
- **D**esire to support the change
- **K**nowledge of how to change
- **A**bility to implement new skills/behaviors
- **R**einforcement to sustain the change

## Value Delivery
Projects exist to deliver value — not just outputs, but outcomes and benefits.

**Value Types:**
- **Tangible Value**: Monetary assets, market share, goodwill
- **Intangible Value**: Reputation, brand recognition, strategic alignment, public benefit

## Benefits Realization
The process of identifying, defining, tracking, and delivering the benefits of a project after completion.

**Benefits Management Plan components:**
- Target benefits
- Strategic alignment
- Timeframe for realizing benefits
- Benefits owner
- Metrics

## SWOT Analysis
A strategic planning tool:
- **S**trengths (internal, positive)
- **W**eaknesses (internal, negative)
- **O**pportunities (external, positive)
- **T**hreats (external, negative)

## Stakeholder Analysis Tools
- **Power/Interest Grid**: Maps stakeholders by power and interest level
- **Stakeholder Engagement Assessment Matrix**: Compares current vs desired engagement levels
  - Unaware, Resistant, Neutral, Supportive, Leading
