# Agile & Hybrid Approaches

## Overview
Agile is an iterative, incremental approach to project management that emphasizes flexibility, collaboration, and rapid delivery of value.

## When to Use Agile
- Requirements are unclear or expected to change
- High uncertainty/complexity
- Need for frequent customer feedback
- Software development, product innovation
- Fast-changing markets

## Agile Manifesto Values
1. **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
2. **Working software** over comprehensive documentation
3. **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
4. **Responding to change** over following a plan

*(Note: items on the right still have value, but items on the left are valued more)*

## 12 Agile Principles (Key Ones for CAPM)
1. Highest priority is customer satisfaction through early and continuous delivery
2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development
3. Deliver working software frequently (weeks, not months)
4. Business people and developers must work together daily
5. Build projects around motivated individuals
6. Face-to-face conversation is most efficient communication
7. Working software is the primary measure of progress
8. Sustainable development pace
9. Continuous attention to technical excellence
10. Simplicity — maximizing work not done
11. Self-organizing teams produce best results
12. Regular reflection and adjustment (retrospectives)

## Scrum Framework

### Roles
- **Product Owner**: Owns product backlog, defines priorities, represents stakeholder/customer voice
- **Scrum Master**: Servant leader, removes impediments, facilitates Scrum process
- **Development Team**: Self-organizing, cross-functional team that delivers increments

### Scrum Events (Ceremonies)
1. **Sprint**: Time-boxed iteration (typically 1-4 weeks) where a "Done" increment is created
2. **Sprint Planning**: Team plans work for the sprint
3. **Daily Scrum (Standup)**: 15-min daily sync — What did I do? What will I do? Any blockers?
4. **Sprint Review**: Demo completed work to stakeholders, gather feedback
5. **Sprint Retrospective**: Team reflects on process, identifies improvements

### Scrum Artifacts
- **Product Backlog**: Prioritized list of all desired work (owned by Product Owner)
- **Sprint Backlog**: Items selected for the current sprint + plan to deliver them
- **Increment**: Sum of all completed backlog items, must meet "Definition of Done"

### Key Scrum Terms
- **User Story**: Short description of a feature from user's perspective
  - Format: "As a [role], I want [feature], so that [benefit]"
- **Story Points**: Relative estimate of effort/complexity (often Fibonacci: 1,2,3,5,8,13)
- **Velocity**: Amount of work a team completes per sprint (measured in story points)
- **Definition of Done (DoD)**: Agreed criteria for when work is complete
- **Backlog Refinement (Grooming)**: Ongoing process of reviewing/prioritizing backlog items
- **Burndown Chart**: Shows remaining work over time (should trend downward)
- **Burnup Chart**: Shows completed work over time (should trend upward)

## Kanban
A visual workflow management method emphasizing continuous flow.

**Key Principles:**
- **Visualize workflow**: Use a Kanban board (To Do, In Progress, Done)
- **Limit Work in Progress (WIP)**: Prevents overloading team
- **Manage flow**: Optimize the flow of work through the system
- **Make policies explicit**: Clear rules for how work moves
- **Continuous improvement**: Kaizen — small, ongoing improvements

**Kanban vs Scrum:**
| Kanban | Scrum |
|--------|-------|
| Continuous flow | Time-boxed sprints |
| No prescribed roles | Defined roles (PO, SM, Team) |
| WIP limits | Sprint backlog fixed at start |
| Pull-based | Sprint commitment |

## Lean
Focuses on maximizing customer value while minimizing waste.

**Types of Waste (Muda):**
- Overproduction
- Waiting
- Transport
- Over-processing
- Inventory
- Motion
- Defects

## Extreme Programming (XP)
Engineering-focused agile methodology emphasizing technical practices:
- Pair programming
- Test-driven development (TDD)
- Continuous integration
- Simple design
- Refactoring

## Hybrid Approaches
Combine predictive and agile elements based on project needs.

**Common Hybrid Models:**
- **Predictive + Agile phases**: Use predictive for planning/procurement, agile for execution
- **Agile with predictive governance**: Agile execution within a stage-gate framework
- **Scrum-fall**: Waterfall planning, Scrum execution

**When to use Hybrid:**
- Parts of the project are well-defined (predictive), parts are uncertain (agile)
- Regulatory requirements need predictive documentation, but delivery benefits from agile iteration
- Large programs with multiple sub-projects of varying uncertainty

## Servant Leadership
A leadership philosophy where the leader's main goal is to serve the team.

**Servant Leader Responsibilities:**
- Remove impediments/blockers
- Facilitate collaboration
- Coach and mentor team members
- Protect team from external distractions
- Foster self-organization

## Agile Estimation Techniques
- **Planning Poker**: Team members estimate story points simultaneously using cards
- **Affinity Estimating**: Group similar-sized items together
- **T-Shirt Sizing**: Rough estimates (XS, S, M, L, XL)

## Adaptive Life Cycle Characteristics
- Short iterations (sprints)
- Frequent stakeholder feedback loops
- Emergent requirements
- Continuous risk assessment
- Cross-functional, self-organizing teams
