# Project Management Fundamentals

## What is a Project?
A project is a temporary endeavor undertaken to create a unique product, service, or result.

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Temporary**: Has a definite beginning and end
- **Unique**: Produces a distinct deliverable
- **Progressive Elaboration**: Details are refined as more info becomes available

## Project vs Operations
| Project | Operations |
|---------|-----------|
| Temporary | Ongoing |
| Unique output | Repetitive output |
| Ends when objectives met | Continues indefinitely |
| Example: Building a bridge | Example: Running a factory |

## Project Management
The application of knowledge, skills, tools, and techniques to project activities to meet project requirements.

## Triple Constraint (Iron Triangle)
The three primary constraints of any project:
1. **Scope**: What work will be done
2. **Time (Schedule)**: When work will be completed
3. **Cost (Budget)**: How much it will cost

**Extended constraints**: Quality, Resources, Risk

**Real-World Example**: If a client wants a bridge built faster (time), you either need more resources/money (cost) or accept reduced scope (fewer features) — you cannot change one constraint without impacting others.

## Project Life Cycle
A project life cycle is the series of phases a project passes through:
1. **Starting the Project** (Initiating)
2. **Organizing and Preparing** (Planning)
3. **Carrying Out the Work** (Executing)
4. **Ending the Project** (Closing)

## Project vs Product Life Cycle
- **Project Life Cycle**: Phases to complete the project (temporary)
- **Product Life Cycle**: Phases a product goes through from conception to retirement (ongoing)

## Organizational Structures

### Functional Organization
- Employees grouped by specialty (e.g., Marketing, Engineering)
- Project Manager has little/no formal authority
- Team members report to Functional Manager
- **PM Authority**: Low

### Matrix Organization
Three sub-types based on PM authority:
- **Weak Matrix**: PM authority low, functional manager dominant
- **Balanced Matrix**: PM and functional manager share authority
- **Strong Matrix**: PM has significant authority, dedicated PM role

### Projectized Organization
- Team members report directly to PM
- PM has high/almost total authority
- Resources dedicated to projects
- **PM Authority**: High

### Organizational Structure Comparison Table
| Structure | PM Authority | Resource Availability | Who Manages Budget |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Functional | Low/None | Low | Functional Manager |
| Weak Matrix | Low | Low | Functional Manager |
| Balanced Matrix | Low to Moderate | Low to Moderate | Mixed |
| Strong Matrix | Moderate to High | Moderate to High | Project Manager |
| Projectized | High/Almost Total | High | Project Manager |

## Project Manager Role
- Leads the project team to achieve objectives
- Balances competing constraints
- Facilitates communication among stakeholders
- Manages risk and issues
- Ensures quality delivery

## PMI Talent Triangle
Three skill sets essential for project managers:
1. **Technical Project Management**: PM knowledge, tools, techniques
2. **Leadership**: Guiding, motivating, directing teams
3. **Strategic and Business Management**: Understanding organizational strategy

## Stakeholders
Any individual, group, or organization that may affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by a project.

**Common Stakeholders:**
- Sponsor
- Customer/Client
- Project Team
- Functional Managers
- End Users
- Suppliers/Vendors
- Government/Regulatory bodies

## Project Charter
A document that formally authorizes a project and gives the PM authority to apply resources.

**Contents typically include:**
- Project purpose/justification
- Measurable objectives
- High-level requirements
- High-level risks
- Summary milestone schedule
- Summary budget
- Stakeholder list
- PM authority level
- Sponsor name and authority

## Work Breakdown Structure (WBS)
A hierarchical decomposition of the total scope of work into smaller, manageable components.

**Key principles:**
- 100% Rule: WBS must capture 100% of the project scope
- Lowest level = Work Packages
- Not a schedule, org chart, or list of activities — it represents deliverables

## Progressive Elaboration
Continuously improving and detailing a plan as more specific information becomes available as the project progresses.

## Rolling Wave Planning
A form of progressive elaboration where near-term work is planned in detail while future work is planned at a higher level.
